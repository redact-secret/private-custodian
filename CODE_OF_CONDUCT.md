# Code of conduct

## Commitment

We want a respectful, accessible environment for people working on evaluation custody and security. Participation is welcome regardless of age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual orientation, or experience.

This is a project-authored policy, not a claim of external certification.

## Expected behavior

- Challenge designs and evidence without attacking people.
- Treat good-faith questions, corrections and incident reports seriously.
- Credit contributors and communicate clearly across language differences.
- Respect accessibility needs, personal boundaries and private information.
- Be honest about custody, review, independence and measurement limitations.
- Follow authorized data-use boundaries even when working privately.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, discrimination, threats, sexualized attention, doxxing, impersonation, intimidation, retaliation, deliberate disruption, and publishing private information without permission are unacceptable.

Pressuring someone to disclose protected material, bypass evaluation budgets, fabricate evidence, or hide an incident is unacceptable. Ordinary technical mistakes and good-faith security criticism are not misconduct.

## Scope

This policy covers issues, PRs, reviews, private project channels, operational collaboration and public representation. It applies before and after any source-code publication.

## Reporting

Contact a repository maintainer through an established private channel. Do not attach protected corpus content or secrets to a conduct report; use restricted evidence references where necessary. Before public participation opens, maintainers must name responsible recipients and an alternate private route for reports involving a maintainer.

Reports are shared only as needed to investigate and respond. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. Good-faith reporters must not face retaliation.

## Enforcement and conflicts

Maintainers may request correction, remove content, pause access or discussion, warn a participant, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently exclude a participant for serious or repeated misconduct. Responses consider severity, recurrence, context and safety.

A conflicted maintainer should recuse themselves where an uninvolved maintainer is available. A participant may request reconsideration through an uninvolved recipient. Operational access may be suspended promptly to contain exposure without prejudging a conduct investigation; keep the incident response and conduct decisions documented separately.
